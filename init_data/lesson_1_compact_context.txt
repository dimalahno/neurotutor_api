LESSON CONTEXT
Title: How to Build a Startup? (A2/B1)
Goals:
- Vocabulary: Learn key vocabulary about startups and markets.
- Grammar: Practice Past Simple vs Present Perfect.
- Pronunciation: Practice English intonation.
- Speaking & Soft Skills: Build and deliver a sales pitch.
- Writing: Write your own sales pitch.

Vocabulary (use actively):
- to be aligned — to be in agreement (example: The founders are aligned on the product vision.)
- risk tolerance — the amount of loss an investor is ready to handle (example: Early-stage investors often have high risk tolerance.)
- pain point — a problem that a business/service can solve (example: We solve a customer pain point in onboarding.)
- scalable — able to grow bigger without breaking (example: We need a scalable solution.)
- pivot — change the course of an existing strategy (example: If the market changes, we can pivot.)
- bring (someone) on board — ask someone to join the team (example: We brought a designer on board.)
- in charge — the main person responsible for something (example: She is in charge of hiring.)
- expenses — costs (example: We need to track expenses carefully.)
- incubator — an organization that helps early-stage startups develop an idea and business plan (example: They joined an incubator for mentorship.)
- accelerator — an organization that helps startups with an MVP grow faster (example: The accelerator helped them scale up.)
- pitch — a short persuasive presentation of an idea/product/company (example: He practiced his pitch for investors.)
- at stake — at risk (example: A lot is at stake in this deal.)

Useful patterns:
- Working at a startup can mean ...
- Compared to a big company, a startup ...
- I'm in the market for ...
- I'm a ... at ...
- I'm building ...
